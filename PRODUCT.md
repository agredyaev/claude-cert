# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Practitioners preparing specifically for:

- Claude Certified Architect — Foundations (CCAR-F)
- Claude Certified Architect — Professional (CCAR-P)

The product assumes technical fluency. It does not teach general AI literacy, conversational Claude usage, basic programming, or unrelated Claude certifications.

## Purpose

Produce exam-ready architectural judgment as efficiently as possible.

The core learning unit is a demonstrated decision:

1. solve an unfamiliar scenario;
2. state the governing constraint and reasoning;
3. commit the answer;
4. compare against per-option rationale;
5. transfer the rule to a different case;
6. revisit misses after spacing;
7. prove mastery on held-out readiness forms.

Foundation precedes Professional as a training policy, not as an official certification prerequisite.

## Scope

### CCAR-F

- 5 official domains
- 30 official task statements
- 6 published scenario families
- multiple-choice and multiple-response practice
- four hands-on labs
- held-out 60-question readiness forms

### CCAR-P

- 7 official domains
- 38 official objectives
- architecture trade-off practice
- one end-to-end capstone spanning design, integration, evaluation, governance, lifecycle, operations, stakeholder communication, and handoff
- two non-overlapping held-out 63-question readiness forms

Developer and Associate material is outside the product scope. Legacy upstream directories may remain in repository history/source storage, but they are not built, published, linked, or treated as product content.

## Learning Method

diagnostic → targeted material → retrieval practice → self-explanation → feedback → hands-on transfer → spaced review → timed mock → held-out readiness.

Principles:

1. Tasks before exposition.
2. Generation before feedback.
3. Discrimination, not answer recognition.
4. Interleaving.
5. Spacing.
6. Transfer across scenarios.
7. Mastery by repeated evidence.
8. Held-out measurement.
9. Architecture over trivia.
10. Practice scores are signals, not predictions.

## Source Hierarchy

1. Official CCAR-F / CCAR-P exam guides define exam scope.
2. Current Anthropic / Claude documentation defines current product behavior.
3. Official prep paths indicate expected depth.
4. Candidate reports identify calibration risks only.
5. Community repositories are comparison benchmarks, not authorities.

If current product behavior differs from the exam-guide snapshot, the material must state the distinction instead of silently choosing one.

## Knowledge Model

The generated learning ontology links:

official domain → official task statement/objective → finer guidance → decision rule / failure mode → scenario or capstone evidence → practice items → held-out readiness items → primary sources.

Generated by utils/build_knowledge_ontology.py into validation/architect/knowledge_ontology.json.

## Content Quality

Content must be concise, technically defensible, objective-linked, scenario-grounded, and free of marketing copy, branding narrative, motivational filler, generic AI introductions, and superficial answer cues.

validation/architect/lint_slop.py blocks repeated option templates and detectable wording/length cues.

## Validation

Required gates:

- official blueprint exactness;
- provenance/scope discipline;
- exam-format coverage;
- objective coverage;
- answer-key integrity and current-doc freshness;
- distractor quality;
- per-option explanation quality;
- scenario/hands-on skill transfer;
- duplication and balance;
- learning-methodology integrity;
- anti-slop lint;
- engine/render tests;
- static site build.

## Interface

The first screen prioritizes next action, task-statement mastery, due reviews, lab/capstone progress, and direct access to material/practice.

Branding is limited to a compact CCAR label. Large promotional headings, slogans, product history, and provenance text do not belong in the training flow.

EN/RU changes interface chrome only. Exam terminology and question content remain English.

## Data and Privacy

The site is static and has no backend or account system. Attempt state, mastery, misses, readiness, labs, and UI preferences stay in browser localStorage.
